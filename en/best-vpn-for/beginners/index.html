Best VPN for beginners

Your first VPN should be hard to get wrong: clear apps, a price you understand and enough time to find out whether you actually use it.

Most people who quit a VPN do so in the first week, usually because the app asked them a question they could not answer. Ease of use is not a nicety here — it is the difference between a subscription and an unused one.

Second comes the exit: how long you have to change your mind, what the real total is for the term, and whether a free tier lets you try the idea before paying anything. All three are printed on this site before you click.

How we chose

Ease of use carries 45% for this guide — the highest share of any — with value at 20%, privacy and speed at 15% each and streaming at 5%. Nothing about the providers changes; only what a beginner needs from them is weighted up.

What this guide weighs

The five published components, re-weighted for this task
Criterion Weight How it is scored
Ease of use 45 % How quickly a non-technical person can install, connect and trust the app; whether servers are labelled by purpose; platform coverage including smart TVs and routers; and whether support is reachable and useful.
Value 20 % The effective monthly cost of the longest term offered, weighted against what the subscription actually delivers — device count, features and network size. Providers whose renewal price roughly doubles score lower, because the real cost of a VPN is the second year, not the first.
Privacy 15 % Jurisdiction and data-retention law; membership of the 5/9/14-Eyes alliances; independent audits of the no-logs claim with a named firm and date; whether servers are RAM-only, which makes retention structurally impossible; documented cases of legal compulsion; how much identifying information signup collects.
Speed 15 % An architectural estimate, not our own benchmark. Protocol support and whether WireGuard is available; RAM-only versus disk infrastructure; server density and proximity to major cities; dedicated streaming and gaming capacity; stability reported in published independent reviews.
Streaming 5 % How consistently the provider restores geo-blocked catalogues across major streaming services, whether it maintains dedicated servers for them, and how often the answer changes. Providers that do not attempt this score low by design, which we state rather than penalise silently.

What a first VPN needs

One screen, one button, sensible defaults. The best first VPN does not ask you to choose a protocol, and it connects you to something reasonable without a tutorial. Everything else can be learned later, on the same account.

Then check the money: the total for the term rather than a monthly headline, the money-back window, and whether the plan covers the devices you already own. We print all three next to every price on this site.

Common questions about beginners

What is the easiest VPN to use?

TunnelBear and ExpressVPN tie at 9.5 for ease of use, with CyberGhost at 9.4 just behind and the longest money-back window. All three are built to work without a manual, which is what easy should mean.

Should a beginner start with a free VPN?

A free tier is a reasonable way to learn what a VPN does — three of our providers offer one. Free tiers are usually slower, smaller and funded somehow, so read what the free plan is funded by before you put anything sensitive through it.

How much should a first VPN cost?

Less than you would notice monthly and not more than you would be annoyed to lose. The real number is the total for the term, which is why every price on this site shows the total next to the monthly figure rather than instead of it.
